Research

Research & Creative Inquiry

Four threads connect my scholarship to my teaching and directing: how opera performs power, how singers learn to act, how text becomes song, and how performance meets technology.

01

Opera & Performance Studies

How opera performs power. My dissertation and papers ask how sexism, imperialism, and exploitation shape the standard repertoire, and what singers and directors can do about it on stage.

  • Sexism and Imperialism in Mozart’s Don Giovanni, Puccini’s Madama Butterfly, and Blitzstein’s Regina: A Performance Study PDF
    Doctoral dissertation, University of Maryland, 2016. A performance study written alongside three roles: Sharpless (Castleton Festival, 2014), Don Giovanni (Maryland Opera Studio, 2015), and Oscar Hubbard (Maryland Opera Studio, 2016).
  • Japonisme, Authenticity, and Anti-Imperialism in Puccini’s Madama Butterfly PDF
    Research paper, University of Maryland. Puccini’s borrowing of Japanese melodies, and the opera’s portrait of American condescension.
Also working on Melody sources of Puccini’s Orientalist operas Verismo and the Giovane Scuola Russian opera of the 19th century
02

Pedagogy of the Singing Actor

How singers learn to act. My teaching and research build a working system for the singing actor: tactical actioning, eye focus and economy of gesture, vulnerability and risk-taking, and motor learning for vocal coordination.

  • On Teaching the Singing Actor
    Teaching philosophy.
  • Preparing “It’s Hard to Speak My Heart”: A Framework for the Singing Actor
    Beat-by-beat actioning, physical gesture, and eye focus applied to one audition piece. April 2026.
  • Daring Greatly on Stage: A Blueprint for Vulnerable Performance
    A five-step framework for risking emotional exposure on stage, drawing on Brené Brown and Stanislavski. July 2026.
  • Larynx Model: Landmark Explorer
    An interactive 3D model of the larynx for teaching vocal anatomy. In development.
  • Acting for Singers curriculum for classical performers Planned
    Drawing on the musical theatre writings of Joe Deer.
Areas of inquiry Tactical actioning Beat and tactics systems in dramatic coaching Eye focus and physical movement Vulnerability and risk-taking Chakra and movement Motor learning in the voice studio
03

Song, Text & Diction

How text becomes song. I study how composers choose, and are chosen by, poetry, and I want to build tools that make foreign-language repertoire easier for singers to learn and stage.

  • “The Words That Chose Him”: Gerald Finzi and the Poetry of Thomas Hardy PDF
    Research paper, University of Maryland. How Hardy’s poetry shaped Finzi’s song composition.
  • Zarzuela libretti of Federico Moreno-Torroba and Pablo Sorozábal Planned
    Complete libretti with IPA transcription and word-for-word translation, to make the repertoire accessible to singers and producers.
Teaching connection Lyric diction: French, Italian, German, English
04

Performance & Technology

Where theatre meets machines. A collaboration between a stage director and a robotics lab, and a continuing interest in how singers work with the technology they will meet in their careers.

  • OperaBot: A Performer Led Robot Theater Collaboration Springer
    Oregon State University, 2019. I directed and operated a robot as the Commendatore in the graveyard scene from Don Giovanni. The CHARISMA robotics lab published the collaboration at ICSR+AI 2025 (Springer, 2026), and I am thanked in the paper as the stage director. Read the story →
  • Amplified performance for musical theatre
    On-mic rehearsal from the first session, so that singers learn the conventions and dramatic possibilities of amplification before tech week.
