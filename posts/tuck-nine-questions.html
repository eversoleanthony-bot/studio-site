Building Angus Tuck: The Nine Questions

Uta Hagen spent her career pushing actors away from generalized, presentational acting, the kind that plays a type instead of a person, and her Nine Questions from Respect for Acting are the discipline she built against it: no answer counts until it’s specific enough to live inside. Answered here for one particular morning in 1893, the way Angus would answer them. The final entry of a three-part character journal.

Uta Hagen’s Nine Questions, laid out in Respect for Acting, are a tool I use to build a character’s inner life. The premise is straightforward: answering these questions forces the actor to define specific circumstances, worldview, and psychological drives, serving as the first line of defense against playing a generic broad type.

It is easy to gesture at grief, age, or fear in the abstract. It is much harder to live inside the specific logistical and emotional details a real person in that situation faces. Hagen designed these questions to force research and imagination to work in tandem, ensuring every choice on stage traces back to something the actor genuinely knows.

While this is the final installment of this series, these questions are best deployed at the very beginning of a rehearsal process. To accommodate a character who has lived for centuries, I have anchored this exercise to one specific morning: the Tuck cottage outside Treegap, 1893, just as Mae wakes Angus to ride out and meet their sons.

Question 1

Who am I?

Not an autobiography. Instead, what are my character traits? What are the marks of my personality? My strengths? Shortcomings? List at least 10 nouns and adjectives that describe the character.

Patient. Dry. Watchful. Protective. Philosophical. Gentle with a child, guarded with a stranger, a watcher. My strength is awareness, I’m weary of the world, but I love and stick with my people and always will. I miss my boys most of the time. I’m weary of the caution required to live our lives, but it has become my new default. I’ve been husband to Mae since the second Sunday of June, 1784, when I was twenty-one. My shortcoming is a pervading sadness that drives me to escape my thoughts through my hobbies and through sleep. Endurance curdles into passivity the moment something calls for reaching first instead of waiting it out, and eighty-five years of practice have made me very good at waiting. Ten words to describe myself: patient, dry, watchful, protective, guarded, plainspoken, weary, tender, aware, resigned.

Question 2

Where am I?

Your environment from the surface of your skin to the edge of the universe, anything pertinent. Country, city, neighborhood, house, room, area of room, clothing. What surrounds me? Animate and inanimate objects. You cannot be too specific.

My cottage outside Treegap, on land that has to look owned by someone but anonymous to the local residents. It was built deliberately to not be worth a second glance, remote enough that the ordinary rhythms of town life, church bells, market days, a doctor who remembers your face, never get the chance to test the lies my whole household runs on. Physically, it’s clean but cluttered, unpainted two-room cottage beside a silent pond, deep in red-fir country. I’m in baggy, rumpled trousers and a plain shirt, the same kind of clothes I’ve worn since before the spring, since there’s no reason to dress for a world I’m not really part of. The room is alive with small, specific things: a mouse living, uninvited and unbothered, in the kitchen table’s drawer; my own forgotten shotgun propped in the corner; dust, spiderwebs, scattered wood shavings, stacked dishes; half-finished wooden dolls, soldiers, and bowls crowding every flat surface, a ship model still waiting for its glue to dry. My skill with the blade has grown exponentially over the years and I still keep some of my earlier work to show me that my skills can grow even if nothing else in this cursed life can. If not for Mae, it would probably be messy as well as cluttered.

The lake is still. Sometimes I’ll go out to fish when I feel like a meal, though eating has become more of a celebratory event rather than a daily necessity of life. Sometimes I’ll go a week without eating anything but I never lose or gain any weight. I also can’t seem to gain any strength. My body composition seems frozen to that day in 1808. I’m grateful I was healthy on that day. A canoe with peeling blue and yellow paint leans against the tree. Sometimes I’ll fish or chop wood to get my body moving and remind me of my past life when such work was a necessity.

Question 3

When is this?

Century, year, season, day, minute. Historical info as well (who is president now? What’s happening in the world? Is there a war raging somewhere? Who is hot on the art scene? What movies, plays, music, celebrities are part of the current environment?)

Morning, in the one week of the decade that organizes my entire existence: the run-up to the first week of August, I’ll see Miles and Jesse this week. Grover Cleveland is president, just sworn in again for a second term. Somewhere in Chicago this year, from what little rumor has reached this far, there’s some kind of great exposition going on, lit up with artificial light. The music people are singing has moved on from the hymns and fiddle tunes of my own childhood, toward parlor songs and ragtime. The reality is my awareness of the world is limited. It’s what comes from the need to stay anonymous.

Question 4

What are my relationships?

Family, loved ones, adversaries, who do you live with? Work with? See everyday? What is your attitude toward people, places, events and things in your life. How do you think about yourself? Anyone you mention in the text, or who mentions me. What do others say about me in the text?

We can’t afford to have records kept on us these days. Eventually, over years people realize we don’t age so we keep moving, remain anonymous, and stay to ourselves. I miss the connection and belonging that comes with community. At least I think I miss it. I’ve become so untrusting of people in defense of keeping this damn secret, I’m not sure I would fit in anymore even if our condition were miraculously cured. I’m grateful I have my Mae. Who knows the state I’d be in without her. I don’t envy my boys who have to endure this existence alone.

Mae is the only person on earth I don’t have to perform anything for, the one relationship built to last exactly as long as I do. She also carries the one unguarded, romantic beat in my entire history. Miles and Jesse are both loved and both kept at a structural distance, not from any lack of feeling but because the decade the two of them spent processing Thomas’s loss differently is the same decade that decided they’d travel alone from now on.

Thomas himself is a relationship that exists only as an absence, a name, a last known age, and roughly seventy-five years, by this point, of not knowing whether he’s even alive.

I think of myself as the one holding the ledger nobody else in this family especially wants to keep. Mae’s own assessment of me is said plainly to my face, more than once: “It’s no use having that dream. Nothing’s going to change.”

Question 5

What are the given circumstances?

Past, present, future, anything that affects your behaviour. What have you lived with all your life as a given, a condition you psychologically or physically accept. What are the immediate preceding events before your monologue or scene begins.

Immediately before this: I was asleep, dreaming the good dream, the one where we’re all in heaven and never heard of Treegap. Mae woke me to tell me she’s riding out to meet the boys. That’s the specific event sitting underneath everything else this morning. Eighty-five years of practicing exactly how to want nothing, need nothing, and be surprised by nothing, because surprise is what gets a family like mine caught. I’m bound to a fixed itinerary, Mae at all times, Miles and Jesse once a decade the first week of August, that leaves almost no room for spontaneity, inside a life that will otherwise never run out of time for it. Nothing can kill me. Everything still depends on what I prevent. I am the only obstacle standing between the spring and a species-level catastrophe, a job no one assigned me and no one can relieve me of.

Question 6

What’s my state of being?

Emotional, physical, mental, spiritual. Will derive from preceding events, and who is in the room with you, who you are talking to.

Something closer to a held breath than to rest. Physically: heavy-limbed, slow to rise, a body that retains the aches and muscle soreness of that day of travel in 1808. Mae is the one in the room with me, already dressing, already halfway to the door. I consider myself to be a man still capable of humor and tenderness rather than someone who has gone fully inert. Beneath it all bubbles something darker and more specific, a genuine theological fear, native to the plain Congregationalist faith I grew up inside, that I may have permanently disqualified my whole family from the heaven I still dream about. Those dreams are a curse in themselves because what god would allow something like this? What falsehoods did my ancestors base their blind faith upon?

Question 7

What do I want?

Right now? From the person I’m talking to? In the near future? In the distant future? In life? If I had the power, how would I arrange events to make me happy?

Right now, from Mae specifically: to be let sleep a few minutes longer, though I already know she won’t allow it. Scaled to this specific morning more broadly: to get through this year’s reunion whole, on schedule, without incident. In the distant future, past this reunion and every reunion left to come: some kind of release, though I couldn’t tell you from what exactly, since the wheel won’t let me back on no matter how much I plead. If I had the power to rearrange anything, I’d put myself, Mae, and the boys (if they want it) back on that wheel. I want the boys to be able to grow old, to experience a family, to enjoy a human life.

Question 8

What’s in my way?

Could be external or internal obstacle. Events, circumstances. Other people’s desires, my own inner conflicts (character flaws, fears, psychological barriers; I’m not ___ enough, I’m afraid of ___.) Objects in the physical world.

My resignation is the real obstacle this morning, a genuine flaw rather than mere circumstance. Other people’s desires work against me too, in their own quiet way: Mae has made her peace with this life more fully than I have, which means she isn’t pushing me to want more than I already have, which means nothing is pushing me at all. Though she, as well as I, would like to find something on the horizon to look forward to. I’m not brave enough, and I’m afraid of what happens the day my caution finally isn’t enough. Even the physical world cooperates with my stillness, a cottage that doesn’t need fixing, a pond that doesn’t need crossing, nothing in reach that requires reaching. My passivity is my poison, but the things I’m protecting will always have to be my obstacle. The cursed spring is the obstacle to fulfillment and I’ll do anything to prevent another person from going through this hell of an existence. I fear that as the world progresses, someday we’ll be found out, held captive, tested, poked and prodded. Greed will always win in the hearts of men, and that is my despair.

Question 9

What am I doing right now to get it?

Physical and verbal behaviour to overcome obstacles and fulfill objective. Verbs!

I carve: wooden spoons, bowls, small toys, models, the clutter of my cottage filled with half-finished objects that never quite need to get finished, since there’s always another eighty-five years to finish them in. I fish. I deflect, with a dry joke, half a second before a real feeling gets visible. Aimed at Mae specifically, this morning: I delay her. I savor her. I follow her lead instead of setting my own. None of it looks like a man defending civilization from itself. That gap, between the size of what I’m carrying and the smallness of what my hands are allowed to do about it, is the whole part. I could always give up. I could give in and stop protecting the spring. I’m only delaying the inevitable after all. Until the spring ceases to exist I’m stuck in this region where I can look after it. Perhaps someday we’ll be able to travel the world, move on to a place where it’s easier to exist anonymously… but right now overcoming this obstacle of immortality and protecting the spring is out of my hands completely. I’m protecting and I’m making sure my family remains cautious and the spring stays protected. No one else must ever drink that water again.
