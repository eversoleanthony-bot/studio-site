# Anthony Eversole, DMA: Research Summary

Prepared October 2026 from the Research page of the redesigned anthonyeversole.com, for use in CV and résumé work. Titles, dates, and citations are as given in Dr. Eversole's own materials. Items marked **Planned** are not completed work.

## In one paragraph

Dr. Eversole is a baritone, stage director, and voice teacher. His scholarship has four threads: how opera performs power (his dissertation on sexism and imperialism in three operas), how singers learn to act (tactical actioning, eye focus, vulnerability, and motor learning), how composers build and borrow (analysis of Finzi, Beethoven, and Puccini), and how theatre meets technology (a robot he directed in a Mozart opera scene, later studied in a robotics paper).

## Thread 2: Pedagogy of the singing actor

A working system for training singers as actors: tactical actioning (a transitive verb for every beat change), eye focus and economy of gesture, vulnerability and shame resilience as the starting point for risk-taking, and motor learning for vocal coordination.

Areas listed on the CV: tactical actioning; beat and tactics systems in dramatic coaching for singers; eye focus and physical movement in singing-actor training; vulnerability and risk-taking for the singing actor; chakra and movement for the singing actor.

## Thread 4: Performance and technology

**OperaBot: a performer-led robot theatre collaboration (Oregon State University, 2019)**

Dr. Eversole was the stage director of *Mozartistry*, twelve Mozart opera scenes performed by the OSU Lyric Opera Ensemble on November 25 and 26, 2019, at Ashbrook Independent School in Corvallis, Oregon. He cast a robot, built over about three months by Dr. Heather Knight's CHARISMA lab (CoRIS Institute, Oregon State University), as the Commendatore (the statue) in the graveyard scene from *Don Giovanni* ("O statua gentilissima"). He directed the scene, operated the robot, and sang its voice live.

The robotics team published the collaboration as a study:

> Swaminathan, J., Alvarado, D., & Knight, H. (2026). OperaBot: A performer led robot theater collaboration. In M. Staffa et al. (Eds.), *Social Robotics + AI: ICSR+AI 2025* (Lecture Notes in Computer Science, vol. 16133, pp. 700-717). Springer, Singapore. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-95-2398-6_48

Dr. Knight presented the work on September 11, 2025, in the Robots on Stage special session of ICSR+AI 2025 in Naples, Italy.

**How to describe his role accurately:** Dr. Eversole is **not an author** of the paper. He is the stage director described in the study and is thanked in its acknowledgments. List it as a creative or research collaboration, for example under "Creative Activity and Interdisciplinary Collaboration," and not under peer-reviewed publications.

Suggested CV line:

> **OperaBot**, Oregon State University CoRIS Institute (Dr. Heather Knight), 2019. Stage director, robot operator, and live voice of the Commendatore in the graveyard scene from *Don Giovanni* (*Mozartistry*, OSU Lyric Opera Ensemble). Documented in: Swaminathan, J., Alvarado, D., & Knight, H. (2026), *Social Robotics + AI: ICSR+AI 2025*, Springer. Acknowledged as director; not an author.

Design lessons the paper draws from the collaboration: let the character suggest and simplify the specifications; use set design for spatial expression and control; give the robot capabilities a human performer lacks; use costume to reduce mechanical complexity. The paper quotes Dr. Eversole on restraint ("Less is more. If you're going for a strong, scary character, the less movement the better.") and on live timing (he rejected a recorded track because "with live musicians your tempo is always going to change").
