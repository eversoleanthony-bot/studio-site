Anthony Eversole

Dr. Anthony Eversole is a baritone, voice pedagogue, and stage director. He has performed and directed widely throughout the United States and taught voice for more than fifteen years. His research interests include actor training for singers, the songs of Gerald Finzi, and gesture and eye-focus efficiency in performance contexts.

Research

Doctoral Dissertation · PDF Sexism and Imperialism in Mozart’s Don Giovanni, Puccini’s Madama Butterfly, and Blitzstein’s Regina: A Performance Study A performance study of three operatic roles and an accompanying document: Sharpless in Madama Butterfly (Castleton Festival, 2014), the title role in Don Giovanni (2015), and Oscar Hubbard in Regina (2016). The document reads the three operas from postcolonial and feminist perspectives, addressing sexism, imperialism, and the exploitation of women and the colonized. University of Maryland · 2016 Research Paper · PDF Japonisme, Musical Borrowing, and Anti-Imperialism in Puccini’s Madama Butterfly Documents Puccini’s borrowing of Japanese popular and folk melodies to build a musically vivid Japan, and considers how the opera’s American characters, who condescend to and exploit colonial Japan, support an anti-imperialist reading. This paper contributed to the development of the doctoral dissertation. University of Maryland Research Paper · PDF The Frailty of Days: A Study of Thomas Hardy’s Impact on Gerald Finzi’s Song Composition Traces Gerald Finzi’s lifelong kinship with Thomas Hardy, whose poetry supplies forty-four of his sixty-four songs for solo voice and piano. Sets the two lives side by side, and examines their shared preoccupation with mortality and time and Finzi’s habit of reading poetry for pleasure and letting musical ideas arise from it.
